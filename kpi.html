Business case

Make reusable design
part of report authoring.

Help report authors communicate performance without starting every KPI layout from a blank canvas.

A KPI can communicate a headline number, a comparison, or a trend. Authors need a starting point that matches that intent; audiences need to distinguish the main value from its supporting information.

The business objective is to make Analytics+ card authoring more approachable and support visual consistency across a report. A reusable preset provides a starting hierarchy, while the actual measures and reporting context determine what belongs in the card.

01 / Overview

A small surface.
Several design decisions.

A KPI card needs to communicate what the number is and how to interpret it.

My Analytics+ work included reusable KPI layouts, presets, and custom icons. This case focuses on the reusable visual structure: how a report author finds a starting arrangement and how that arrangement makes information readable.

The supplied Power BI screens show the Card ribbon and KPI Preset pane beside the report canvas. Measures are already present in the field well. This is a preset-selection experience, not a data-preparation workflow.

Reading the evidence

The preset tiles use sample values that differ from the adjacent report canvas. A highlighted tile is a visible selection state, not proof of an applied result or a matched before-and-after example. The shared Figma link required sign-in; this case uses the supplied images, not inspected prototype interactions.

02 / Simple presets

Establish the value.
Give it a clear label.

A clear starting hierarchy matters even when the card contains one metric.

The first supplied screen ↑ shows the Simple tab with centered and left-aligned arrangements, a side-accent treatment, and image placeholders. Each preview presents an alternative balance between the value and its label.

The design intent is to make those arrangements recognizable before the author commits to further formatting. The screenshot documents the visible choices, not every available preset or its interaction behavior.

03 / Variance presets

Keep the headline.
Add the comparison.

Comparison information should explain the main value without competing with it.

The Variance tab groups layouts that combine a headline metric with differences, percentages, arrows, and colored comparison blocks. The previews vary the position and emphasis of those elements while retaining a distinct main value.

The selected canvas example and the preset thumbnails use different values and comparison counts. They illustrate the design space, not a demonstration that the selected thumbnail generated that exact report card.

The Advanced tab is visible in both supplied screens. Its detailed contents and behavior are not shown, so this walkthrough does not invent an Advanced selection state.

04 / Preset library

A family of patterns.
Not one fixed card.

Reuse the visual logic while allowing different reporting needs.

The supplied design sheet brings together simple metric arrangements, icon placements, variance treatments, and compact trend examples. It illustrates how a common value-and-context hierarchy can extend across different card compositions.

Across my broader KPI design work, the confirmed deliverables included 30+ KPI layouts, 20+ presets, and 70+ custom icons. Reusable KPI templates shipped. These are scope counts, not a count of the examples in this image or a claim that every pictured variation shipped.

05 / Reflection

Consistency is more
than matching colors.

A reusable preset needs a repeatable reading order, not only a repeatable appearance.

The main design principle is to keep the metric recognizable while giving comparisons and supporting visuals a deliberate place. Separating Simple and Variance families makes the reporting intent part of browsing, rather than asking every author to rebuild the hierarchy.

Role, delivery & evidence

Role: reusable KPI layouts, presets, icons, and comparison hierarchy. Reusable templates shipped.

Evidence: Simple and Variance design screens and the layout library. Deliverable counts describe design scope; preview values illustrate layouts.

Review: broader Analytics+ requirements walkthroughs and implementation-fidelity review. Preset-specific usability measurements and historical iteration records are not included.
