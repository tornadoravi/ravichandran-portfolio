Business case

Help the audience
understand the meaning.

Support the explanation of data, not only its display.

Authors need to connect observations to a specific visual or the wider report. Audiences need a clear reference for the point being discussed.

Design rationale

Pain points & solution highlights.

Communication challenges grounded in the confirmed scope and documented capabilities.

  1. 01 / Pain point

    An explanation needs a reference.

    A note about one value can be confused with a statement about the whole report if their contexts are not distinguished.

    Solution highlightKeep data-point and canvas-level annotation contexts distinct, so the intended reference remains understandable.

    See annotations →
  2. 02 / Pain point

    Notes need a shared reading context.

    Explanations attached to separate elements also need to make sense as part of the wider report.

    Solution highlightThe documented Comments pane consolidates element-level and dashboard-level annotations. This is product context, not a claim of full panel ownership.

    See comments context →
  3. 03 / Pain point

    A dense report divides attention.

    An audience may need guidance toward the visual and explanation currently being discussed.

    Solution highlightPresentation and slideshow design focus on directing attention while maintaining the connection to the wider data story.

01 / Overview

A chart shows the data.
The story needs context.

Data storytelling connects what the audience can see with what the presenter needs to explain.

My Analytics+ contributions included annotations, presentation mode, and slideshow interactions. This case focuses on that communication layer: relating explanations to visuals and directing attention during a presentation.

Report creation, layout, and templates are covered in the separate Storyboard authoring case →. Its six screenshots remain authoring evidence, not presentation-mode screenshots.

02 / Annotations

Explain the point.
Preserve its reference.

An explanation is useful only when its relationship to the visual is clear.

The design intent behind my annotation work was to keep an observation close to the data it describes. A note about a specific value needs a different reference from a statement about the whole report.

Analytics+ documentation distinguishes canvas-level and data-point annotations, with rich-text formatting and annotation indicators. Those capabilities provide context for the design problem; they are not a claim that I designed every documented setting.

SPECIFIC OBSERVATION

Point-level meaning

Keep the intended data reference recognizable when explaining a particular value or contrast.

WIDER CONTEXT

Report-level meaning

Give broader explanations a clear place without implying that they describe one data point.

03 / Comments

Individual notes.
A connected explanation.

The report needs a way to make its explanations understandable together.

The official annotation guide describes an All comments view and a Comments pane that collects element-level and dashboard-level annotations. In this context, “comments” refers to the documented annotation experience, not an assumed threaded discussion system.

The design consideration is the relationship between a note and its source: a consolidated explanation should still make clear which element or report-level context it describes. The concept illustration expresses this relationship, not an exact product layout.

Comments-panel capabilities provide product context; my contribution here covers annotations, presentation mode, and slideshow interactions.

04 / Presentation

From the overview
to the point that matters.

Presentation changes the question from “What can I configure?” to “What should the audience follow?”

My contribution included presentation mode and slideshow interactions. The intent was to support a deliberate explanation of the data rather than leave the audience to interpret every element at once.

The official guide also documents animated annotations and a Present option in the Comments pane. That is a vendor-described capability, not a recording of my prototype or proof that the conceptual sequence below was implemented.

The diagram illustrates the communication principle: establish the wider report, focus on a relevant region, and explain the observation. It intentionally does not invent playback controls, keyboard shortcuts, or transition timing.

05 / Reflection

The explanation
is part of the experience.

The visual and the narrative should support each other.

Annotations give an observation a reference. A comments view provides a place to consider explanations together. Presentation focuses the audience on the part of the story being discussed. Keeping these relationships clear is the central design intent of this case.

Role, delivery & evidence

Role: annotations, presentation mode, and slideshow interactions. Comments documentation is product context.

Evidence: nine design screens and two separately labeled concept illustrations. Screens document design states, not recorded playback or every intermediate transition.
